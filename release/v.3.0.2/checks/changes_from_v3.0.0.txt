Changes of v.3.0.2 against v.3.0.0 (15 November 2026)

Two row filler cells left out, nothing else.

   place   filler left out                                     new first cell of the row   its NWell to the macro NWell corner
   1       sg13cmos5l_fill_1, r0 at (1631.04, 2993.76) um      sg13cmos5l_tiehi            0.629 um
   2       sg13cmos5l_fill_4, r0 at (3120.96, 2993.76) um      sg13cmos5l_dfrbpq_1         1.92 um

Why: at the top right corners of two RM_IHPSG13_1P_64x16_c2 macros the macro NWell ended at y 2995.025 um and the
NWell of the first cell of the row beside it began at y 2995.510 um: a gap of 0.485 um against 0.62 um of rule NW.b.
In both places that first cell was a row filler. Fillers have no devices and no nets. In the thinned file, the NWell
box that the filler edge fix of v.2.1.0 kept for the filler at place 1 is gone with it.

Proof that nothing else changed: the full-fill file and the layout without fill of v.3.0.2 were each compared with
those of v.3.0.0 cell by cell (KLayout LayoutDiff, all shapes and instances): 2 differences each, the two filler
instances, present only in v.3.0.0. The thinned one-file GDS was then made from the full-fill file by the same
script as in v.3.0.0.

v.3.0.1 is not a release: it joined the wells with two drawn NWell rectangles whose joints were narrower than
0.62 um (rule NW.a); its candidate branch is marked as withdrawn.

What this means for the checks:
- run again on the files of v.3.0.2, because they read NWell, the fillers or the whole file: the IHP minimum rule set
  on the GDS of this release and on the layout without fill, density, LVS, the NWell rules and the NWell gap
  measurement;
- carried from v.3.0.0, because their inputs are unchanged: the routed database, the netlist and the signal metal are
  the same, and the supply rails of the rows are drawn in the top cell over each whole row, so leaving out a filler
  removes only its own short rail piece at the start of the row. These are open inputs and wiring, simulation of the
  routed netlist, timing with the routed wires, the router's antenna check, the net trace of the metal, and the
  voltage drop.
