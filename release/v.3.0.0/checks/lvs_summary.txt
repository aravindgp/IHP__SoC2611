LVS of the layout of this release (14 November 2026)

Tool and deck: KLayout 0.30.5, IHP SG13CMOS5L LVS deck of IHP-Open-PDK dcb03b1e2d24250bccbb2e8783d55d2b4f734f14, deep mode.
Result: netlists match. 0 warnings, 0 errors. Run time 9,395 s.

What was compared
  circuits (cell definitions) matched:        67 of 67
  instances of the chip top matched:          354,825
  nets of the chip top matched:               354,306 (743 of them from groups of interchangeable nets, KLayout's
                                              note for equivalent nets such as unloaded outputs)
  devices in the standard-cell definitions:   412

Against the candidate v.2.5.0 (354,762 instances, 354,656 nets): 63 more instances (12 buffers at antenna nets,
22 drive buffers of the repaired signals, 29 antenna diodes) and 350 fewer nets. The net count shows the repair:
the 384 buffer inputs that had no connection were 384 nets of one pin each; they are now pins of the 22 repaired
signals, and 34 new buffers add 34 nets.

Scope
  - Every standard cell with its devices, the antenna diode cells included, and every connection of the chip top:
    cell to cell, cell to SRAM pin, cell to pad, pad ring and power straps, the new supply bars and ties included.
  - SRAM macros and pad cells are black boxes with their pins. They are IHP hard IP; inside them only the drawn
    metal and the vias are kept, so the connections to their pins and through their rails are the real ones.
  - The layout is the one before fill. Fill shapes are on their own datatypes, and the minimum rule set, which is
    clean on the released GDS, checks their spacing to the drawn metal.
  - The seal ring instance is left out. It holds no device and no signal net.

What LVS cannot see, and what covers it since this release
  LVS compares the layout with the routed netlist. A pin that has no net in the netlist has no net in the layout
  either, so the two agree: that is how the 384 open inputs passed LVS in every earlier file. The check of every
  input pin for a net (open_inputs.txt) and the simulation of the routed netlist (simulation_summary.txt) cover it.

Changes that made the run finish on this chip (the unchanged flow did not finish in more than 15 hours)
  View of the layout:
   1. the seal ring instance is left out: its box covers the chip, and the hierarchical net extraction pairs it
      with every instance;
   2. inside SRAM and pad cells only metal drawing, pin and text shapes and via drawing shapes are kept: the bit
      cells carry metal-resistor markers that the deck turns into 189 million resistors;
   3. via cells and row filler cells are dissolved into the top cell (4.72 million instances): the align and
      simplify steps of the deck handle device-less cells one instance at a time.
  Netlist: SRAM and pad subcircuits emptied with their pins kept, row filler instances removed, subcircuits the
      chip top does not reach removed (the align step would flatten the unused SRAM-internal subcircuits).
  Deck, rule text otherwise unchanged: the substrate is one global net instead of a chip-wide polygon, the NMOS
      bulk terminal is taken on its own layer, and a metal layer is used as drawn when no filler, slit or resistor
      marker shape exists on it. The changed and the unchanged deck give the same verdict on eight standard cells
      and the same netlist structure on an SRAM macro.
