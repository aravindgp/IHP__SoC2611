Root cause of the residue, found this morning: the RM_IHPSG13 pins are 0.26 um Metal2 squares on one macro edge
(south for R0/MY, north for MX/R180). 30 of the last 44 crossing nets were SRAM data-pin nets whose only load sat on
the far side of the macro, because the pocket in front of the pin edge was an H13 placement blockage; the router then
had no legal path round the 417 um macro. Fix: `pins_side.py` uses the true pin edge and moves those loads into the
pocket (`eco_sidemove.tcl DROP_BLOCKAGES=1`); the remaining 17 nets (long nets across the stack, clock leaves feeding
macro clock pins above and below a macro, cells directly above and below a small macro) got explicit routes through
the 60 um channels (`guide_channels.py` + `eco_guides.tcl`). First routed state with no supply short.

## Open: a congestion knot of signal-to-signal shorts
The totals of every round carried ~1,200-1,600 "Short" markers between signal nets outside the macros. On round H
1,132 of 1,222 lie in one 500 um square around the dp_sink3 / dp_fetch SRAM block (x 2500-3000, y 2500-3000), 1,076 of
them within 10 um of a macro edge: the 20 um gaps between facing macro back edges and the 60 um channels carry more
traffic than they have tracks (148 nets in the knot; 97 cross the 20 um gap north-south, 31 are macro buses fanning to
several slices, 61 cell pins sit inside one channel). Passes alone do not dissolve such a knot (H10: 139 left in one
cell after 64 passes). In progress: a 32-pass clean-up on the zero-supply-short database, and a local floorplan ECO
that lifts the three small slices above the gap by 40 um (gap 20 -> 60 um) with re-route of the knot nets
(`eco_macroshift.tcl`); measured in 20-minute loops (plan, 3 route passes, count).
